novice:
  Single-step arithmetic or simple big-O comparison (e.g. "which is faster, O(n log n) or O(n²)?").
  One formula, one variable.
  Direct calculation with no interpretation needed.

intermediate:
  Multi-step estimation with 2-3 variables (e.g. "estimate storage for 1M users with profile pictures").
  Requires unit conversion and back-of-envelope reasoning.
  Probability questions with one level of indirection.

expert:
  Estimation with uncertainty bounds and tradeoffs (e.g. "estimate cost for a video processing pipeline — include storage, compute, and bandwidth").
  Statistical reasoning with confidence intervals.
  Boolean logic simplification with multiple interdependent conditions.

frontier:
  Quantitative reasoning under incomplete information — must identify what data is missing and make reasonable assumptions.
  Probability chain with conditional dependencies.
  Geometric reasoning about spatial data structures applied to an unfamiliar problem domain.
  "How would you measure X" when no direct measure exists.
